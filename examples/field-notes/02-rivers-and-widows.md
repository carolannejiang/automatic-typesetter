# Rivers and Widows

Typesetters have a private bestiary of errors. A *river* is a crack of
white space that snakes down through justified text when the word spacing
of several lines happens to align. A *widow* is the last line of a
paragraph stranded alone at the top of a page; an *orphan* is a first line
abandoned at the bottom. A *stack* is the same word ending three lines in a
row, like a stutter.

> The details are not the details. They make the design.
>
> — attributed to Charles Eames

Digital typesetting engines fight these creatures automatically, with
varying success. Justification needs hyphenation the way an arch needs a
keystone: forbid breaking words and the spaces between them must stretch,
and the rivers begin to flow.

Some practical numbers, for the curious:

| Setting | Typical book value |
|---|---|
| Body size | 10.5–12 pt |
| Leading | 120–145% of body |
| Line length | 60–70 characters |
| First-line indent | 1–1.5 em |

The strangest convention to modern eyes is that book paragraphs are not
separated by blank lines. The web taught us space-between-paragraphs; print
uses a first-line indent and no space at all, except where the author
intends a real pause — and that pause is marked with a small ornament or a
blank line called, wonderfully, a *line white*.
