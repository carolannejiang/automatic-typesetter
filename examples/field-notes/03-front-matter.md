# Front Matter, Explained

Open any hardcover and you pass through a small ceremony before the story
starts: a half-title, maybe a frontispiece, the title page, the copyright
page in its tiny type, a dedication, a table of contents. This is *front
matter*, and its pages are numbered in roman numerals precisely so that the
real text can begin, cleanly, at page 1.

The conventions run deep:

- Chapters traditionally open on a *recto* — the right-hand page — even if
  that means leaving a left-hand page blank.
- The first page of a chapter carries no running head, and often drops its
  folio to the foot of the page.
- Verso running heads name the book or the author; recto running heads name
  the chapter.

A converted blog inherits all of this surprisingly well. Posts become
chapters. The feed's title becomes the book's. The copyright page notes
where the words first appeared. And the archive — that infinite scroll —
becomes finite, which is the whole point. A book ends, and being able to
end is a feature no website has ever shipped.
